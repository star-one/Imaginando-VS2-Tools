VS2 Tools / Shadertoy to Material

Shadertoy to VS2 Material converter

Turn a shader from Shadertoy into a Material for VS2 (Visual Synthesizer) by Imaginando. Paste or upload the code, choose which numbers in it should become knobs on VS2, and download a plain and an audio-reactive .frag file. Everything runs in your browser: your code is never uploaded anywhere.

Check the licence first. This page keeps the credit in the converted file, but following the licence is up to you.

1. The shader

    What converts: single-pass shaders, which is most of them. What does not: shaders that read other buffers (Buffer A to D, feedback), because a Material cannot remember the previous frame; textures, video, webcam and cubemaps (you can swap in noise, sound or black); keyboard input; and sound or VR shaders.

    2. Credit

    These go into the manifest and the header comment of the converted files.

    3. Textures

    This shader reads from texture channels. VS2 Materials have no textures, so choose what each one should be. If a channel shows another buffer on Shadertoy, the shader cannot be converted faithfully: choose black or noise.

    4. Numbers and preview

    These are the numbers in the shader's code. Try one: drag the slider in its row and watch the preview change. Nothing is committed: the slider only changes the preview. When a number turns out to be worth keeping, pick a knob for it in the last column, and that knob will start at the value you tried. VS2 has seven knobs per Material.

    LineWhereTry itKnob

    The preview runs the converted code in your browser. It is an approximation: VS2 may differ, heavy shaders may be slow here, and the simulated music is only a rough drumbeat. At most eight numbers can be tried at once; trying a ninth puts the oldest back.

    5. Knobs

    Names and ranges are filled in from the numbers you linked; change any of them if you like. A knob's value is the value of the first number linked to it (other numbers on the same knob keep their proportions), and "Starts at" is where the knob sits when the Material loads. In the audio version, the three boxes on the right say how strongly bass, mids and highs move each knob: 1 moves it a quarter of its range, negative numbers move it down, and 0 does nothing.

    KnobNameLowestHighestStarts atNumbersBassMidsHighs

    Only needed if the shader uses the mouse (iMouse). The two knobs then stand in for the mouse position. Otherwise the mouse is left at zero.

    6. The Material

    The speed multiplier is SPEED_SCALE in the code: the shader's clock runs at VS2's Speed knob times this number. The project default is 8; set it to 1 to run at the speed the shader was written for.

    The audio version

    If fewer than seven knobs are in use, react is simply added. If all seven are in use, it takes the chosen slot and the knob that was there becomes a fixed number. react also scales the sound the shader reads, if it reads any.

    Flash the whole picture brighter with the music (0 does nothing):

    Audio frequency bands

    Give each band's lowest and highest frequency in Hz. VS2 does not say how frequencies are laid out across its FFT texture, so the last three boxes are an assumption you can correct: the frequency at the left edge, the frequency at the right edge, and whether the spacing is logarithmic or even. The defaults assume 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, logarithmic.
