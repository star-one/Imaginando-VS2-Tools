VS2 Tools / SVG to Material

SVG to VS2 Material converter

Turn a vector drawing into a Material for VS2 (Visual Synthesizer) by Imaginando. Paste or upload an SVG, choose how the Material should behave, and download a plain and an audio-reactive .frag file. Everything runs in your browser: your SVG is never uploaded anywhere.

2. How the shape is converted

The shape is simplified to fit. More segments are more faithful but slower on the GPU, because every pixel checks every segment. About 300 is comfortable on a laptop.

3. The Material

The speed multiplier is SPEED_SCALE in the code: everything that moves runs at the VS2 Speed knob times this number. The project default is 8.

The seven knobs

Each Material gets seven knobs in VS2. Set the range and the starting value of each one. The audio version swaps knob 5 for react (audio sensitivity), and keeps the effect amount as a constant.

Audio reactions (audio version only)

Choose how bass, mids and highs change the picture. Each box is how strongly that band drives that setting: 0 does nothing, 1 is the standard amount, 2 is double, up to 3. A band can drive several settings, and a setting can follow several bands. The knob react then scales everything.

Audio frequency bands (audio version only)

The audio version listens to three bands of the sound. Give each band's lowest and highest frequency in Hz. VS2 does not say how frequencies are laid out across its FFT texture, so the last three boxes are an assumption you can correct: the frequency at the left edge of the texture, the frequency at the right edge, and whether the spacing between them is logarithmic or even. The defaults assume 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz, logarithmic. The README explains how to check this against your own copy of VS2.

In VS2, create a new shader Material in the Material Editor and paste the code in (or import the file, if your version of VS2 offers that). The comment block at the top of the file names the knobs. See the VS2 manual's Material Editor section for details.

What converts well: paths (all commands, including curves and arcs), rectangles, circles, ellipses, lines, polylines and polygons, with transforms and groups. What does not: text (convert it to outlines first), gradients, filters, clipping and masks, images, and per-shape colours and stroke widths. Colour and line thickness come from the Material's knobs.
